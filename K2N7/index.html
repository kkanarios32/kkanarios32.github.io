Definition. Gated Linear Unit (GLU) [K2N7]

  • July 14, 2025
  • Kellen Kanarios

A gated linear unit is an activation function combined with an elementwise multiplication i.e.

  • \(\mathrm {ReGLU} = \mathrm {ReLU}(\mathbf {x}) \otimes \mathbf {V} \mathbf {x},\)
  • \(\mathrm {SwiGLU} = \mathrm {Swish}(\mathbf {x}) \otimes \mathbf {V} \mathbf {x},\)
where \(\mathrm {ReLU}\) is the ReLU activation and \(\mathrm {Swish}\) is the Swish activation respectively.

Remark. The matrix \(\mathbf {V}\) introduces additional learnable parameters. Therefore, an architecture that uses gating typically reduces their other parameters by a factor of \(\frac {2}{3}\).

Card. Gating splits the up-projection in two [Y4Y8]

  • August 18, 2026
  • Kellen Kanarios

For one Transformer layer with a gated MLP, what are the parameter count and training FLOPs — and why is the coefficient three rather than two?

Answer Parameters: \(3DF\), from \(W_{in1}[D,F]\), \(W_{in2}[D,F]\), \(W_{out}[F,D]\). Training FLOPs: \(18BTDF\) — three matmuls at \(6BTDF\) each, by the \(6NPM\) rule. Where the third matrix comes from. A Gated Linear Unit (GLU) splits the up-projection into two matrices whose outputs are multiplied elementwise, \(\sigma (AW_{in1}) \odot (AW_{in2})\), so one of them acts as a gate on the other. LLaMA, DeepSeek and most current models do this. A non-gated MLP has a single \(W_{in}\) and so \(2DF\) params — and typically scales \(D\) and \(F\) up to compensate, which is why the two families cost about the same at equal quality. The gate itself is \(O(BTF)\) — elementwise, no contraction, and negligible against \(BTDF\).
