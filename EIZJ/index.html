Card. Two chips split \(X[B, D]\) and \(Y[D, F]\) along \(D\), each computing a partial sum and exchanging it. When is this compute-bound, and why is the threshold surprising? [EIZJ]

  • August 6, 2026
  • Kellen Kanarios
Answer. Each chip does half the contraction, \(T_{\mathrm {math}} = BDF / 1.97 \times 10^{14}\), then ships its \([B, F]\) partial sum across the interconnect, \(T_{\mathrm {comms}} = 2BF / 4.5 \times 10^{10}\). Compute-bound means \[\frac {BDF}{2BF} = \frac {D}{2} > \frac {1.97 \times 10^{14}}{4.5 \times 10^{10}} = 4377 \implies D > 8755\] The surprise is that the threshold depends on \(D\), not \(B\): the batch scales FLOPs and bytes-sent identically and cancels out. Same roofline machinery as a single-chip matmul, but because the relevant bandwidth is now the network, a different dimension decides whether Tensor Parallelism pays for itself — the contracted dimension has to be large enough to hide the exchange.
