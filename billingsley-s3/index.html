Exercise 3.2. Measurability as agreement of inner and outer measure

  • November 17, 2024
  • Kellen Kanarios

Let \(P\) be a probability measure on a field \(\mathscr {F}_{0}\), define \(P^{\star }(A)\) for every subset \(A\) of \(\Omega \) by (3.1), and denote also by \(P\) the extension of \(P\) to \(\mathscr {F}=\sigma (\mathscr {F}_{0})\). Show that \(A\) is \(P^*\)-measurable if and only if \(P^*(A) = P_*(A)\).

Solution.

Suppose \(P^*(A) = P_*(A)\) and fix \(E \subset \Omega \). We must show

\[P^*(E \cap A) + P^*(E \cap A^c) = P^*(E)\]

The preceding part of the exercise supplies \(\overline {A}, \underline {A} \in \mathscr {F}\) with \(\underline {A} \subset A \subset \overline {A}\), and the hypothesis collapses their measures onto each other:

\[P(\overline {A}) = P^*(A) = P_*(A) = P(\underline {A})\]

Approximate \(E\) the same way — there is \(B \in \mathscr {F}\) with \(E \subset B\) and \(P^*(E) = P(B)\). Then

\[P^*(E \cap A) \leq P^*(B \cap \overline {A}) = P(B \cap \overline {A})\]

and

\[P^*(B \cap A) \leq P^*(B \cap \underline {A}^c) = P(B \cap \underline {A}^c)\]

so that

\[P^*(E \cap A) + P^*(E \cap A^c) \leq P(B \cap \underline {A}^c) + P(B \cap \overline {A}) = P^*(E)\]

as desired.

Remark. The difficulty is bookkeeping, not measure theory: three objects live on three different domains, and conflating them is what makes the problem look impenetrable.
  1. \(P\) is defined on the initial field \(\mathscr {F}_0\), the closure over finite unions and complements of \(\Omega \).
  2. \(P^*\) is defined on the whole powerset \(\mathcal {P}(\Omega )\), but is not a measure there. In particular \(E\) need not lie in \(\mathscr {F}\), which is exactly why the argument has to route through \(B\).
  3. \(P\) (abusing notation) is \(P^*\) restricted to the \(P^*\)-measurable subsets of \(\Omega \).
The tempting shortcut — apply finite additivity and conclude everything is \(P^*\)-measurable — fails because \(P^*\) is countably subadditive only on the \(P^*\)-measurable sets, not on \(\mathcal {P}(\Omega )\).

    Exercise 1.1. No infinite fair independent sequence on a discrete space

    • November 17, 2024
    • Kellen Kanarios

    Show that a discrete probability space cannot carry an infinite sequence of independent events \(A_1, A_2, \ldots \), each of probability \(1/2\).

    Solution.

    Denote the sample space \(\mathcal {X}\). Independence of \(A_1\) and \(A_2\) splits it into four cells of probability \(1/4\) apiece:

    \[P(A_1 \cap A_2) + P(A_1 \cap A_2^c) + P(A_1^c \cap A_2) + P(A_1^c \cap A_2^c) = 1\]

    Every \(x \in \mathcal {X}\) lies in exactly one of the four, so \(\mathbb {P}(\{x\}) \leq 1/4\). Running the same construction on \(A_1, \ldots , A_n\) gives \(n\) disjoint cells and the bound \(\mathbb {P}(\{x\}) \leq 1/n\) for every \(x\); the sequence is infinite, so \(n\) is unbounded and \(\mathbb {P}(\{x\}) = 0\).

    But the space is discrete, so all of its mass sits on its atoms:

    \[\sum _{x \in \mathcal {X}} \mathbb {P}(\{x\}) = 1\]

    A countable sum of zeros cannot be one, and we have our contradiction.
