Definition. ReduceScatter [CAGW]

  • August 12, 2026
  • Kellen Kanarios

The collective that resolves partial sums along a mesh axis and leaves the result sharded rather than replicated: \([A, B]\{U_X\} \to [A_X, B]\). It is the reduction half of an AllReduce, and the transpose of an AllGather. Same ring pass, same cost \(V / W_{\mathrm {ici}}\), except each device sums the shard arriving from its left neighbour into its own copy instead of merely keeping it.

Notation. Because it introduces a subscript, you have to say where it goes: \(\mathrm {ReduceScatter}_{X,K}\) reduces along mesh axis \(X\) and shards the result along \(K\). That freedom is the whole argument for preferring it. Namely, whenever the consumer can accept a sharded input, stopping at the ReduceScatter costs half what an AllReduce does and defers the gather, possibly forever.

The mechanism is easiest to see as a schedule. Each device starts holding a full-size buffer that is only a partial sum, and on every step forwards one chunk rightward and folds in the chunk arriving from its left. After \(N-1\) steps device \(b\) holds chunk \(b\) summed over all \(N\) partials and has forwarded the rest away, which is also why the resident memory falls by \(N\).

Question. How do we know what chunk to send to who?
Answer.It is actually remarkably easy. If there are \(N\) devices, then you just compute which device is \(N - 1\) hops away from you. You send that chunk to your right neighbor. Each chunk then propagates through the ring and will end up with the completed chunked sum on the correct device

Note that the steps are not \(N-1\) rounds of moving a whole buffer: every link carries one chunk in the same direction at the same time, so the cost is \(N-1\) chunk-times, and the \(N\) in the chunk size cancels the \(N-1\) in the step count. Nothing is ever broadcast, which is the sense in which the ring is optimal and the reason a ReduceScatter is not merely an AllReduce with a slice at the end.

Card 1. ReduceScatter is AllGather transposed [HIDN]

  • August 12, 2026
  • Kellen Kanarios

ReduceScatter: signature, relationship to AllGather, and cost?

Answer Signature \([A, B]\{U_X\} \to [A_X, B]\) — it resolves partial sums along \(X\) and leaves the result sharded along a dimension of your choosing. It introduces a subscript, which is why you have to say where: \(\mathrm {ReduceScatter}_{X,K}\). Mechanism: the same ring pass as an AllGather, except you sum the incoming shards instead of keeping them. Cost is therefore identical, \(V / W_{\mathrm {ici}}\). Duality: AllGather and ReduceScatter are transposes of each other, hence each other's derivative — an AllGather in the forward pass means a ReduceScatter on the reverse-mode gradients, and vice versa. (Broadcast is \(u \otimes I\), reduce is \(u^\top \otimes I\).)

Card 2. AllReduce is two collectives, not one [MJ7A]

  • August 12, 2026
  • Kellen Kanarios

AllReduce: signature, decomposition, and cost relative to an AllGather?

Answer Signature \([A_X, B]\{U_Y\} \to [A_X, B]\) — it removes the "unreduced" suffix, leaving every device on the axis holding the same fully-summed value, unsharded along \(Y\). It is not really a primitive: an AllReduce is a ReduceScatter followed by an AllGather. \[A[I_X, J]\{U_Y\} \xrightarrow {\ \mathrm {ReduceScatter}_{Y,J}\ } A[I_X, J_Y] \xrightarrow {\ \mathrm {AllGather}_Y\ } A[I_X, J]\] Cost \(2V / W_{\mathrm {ici}}\), exactly \(2\times \) an AllGather — which is the practical argument for stopping at the ReduceScatter whenever the consumer can accept a sharded output. The AllGather can then be deferred, or skipped entirely.
