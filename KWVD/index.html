Card. Collectives are primitives parameterised by axis name [KWVD]

What do psum[axes=('x',) axis_index_groups=None] and shard_map look like in a jaxpr?

Answer Collectives are ordinary primitives parameterised by an axis name; they only mean anything inside a mapped context that binds that name. shard_map appears as 
shard_map[jaxpr={...} mesh=Mesh('x': 8, axis_types=(Auto,))
          in_specs=(P('x',),) out_specs=(P(),) check_vma=True]
with the per-device program nested inside (vmap leaves no primitive behind). Inside that body a collective whose result is already replicated may print as psum_invariant rather than psum — same reduction, tagged as producing an axis-invariant value. So the jaxpr level is logical and the physical level is elsewhere. Here you see a named axis and per-device shapes; the device assignment appears only later, as HLO sharding attributes (A sharding annotation is a tiling plus a device order) and concrete all-reduce instructions (replica_groups says who reduces with whom). That separation is why the axis name is the right thing to reason about: it is stable, whereas which devices land in which replica_groups depends on the mesh and the compiler (Sharding).
