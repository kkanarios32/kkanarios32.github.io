Exercise 1.1. No infinite fair independent sequence on a discrete space

  • November 17, 2024
  • Kellen Kanarios

Show that a discrete probability space cannot carry an infinite sequence of independent events \(A_1, A_2, \ldots \), each of probability \(1/2\).

Solution

Denote the sample space \(\mathcal {X}\). Independence of \(A_1\) and \(A_2\) splits it into four cells of probability \(1/4\) apiece:

\[P(A_1 \cap A_2) + P(A_1 \cap A_2^c) + P(A_1^c \cap A_2) + P(A_1^c \cap A_2^c) = 1\]

Every \(x \in \mathcal {X}\) lies in exactly one of the four, so \(\mathbb {P}(\{x\}) \leq 1/4\). Running the same construction on \(A_1, \ldots , A_n\) gives \(n\) disjoint cells and the bound \(\mathbb {P}(\{x\}) \leq 1/n\) for every \(x\); the sequence is infinite, so \(n\) is unbounded and \(\mathbb {P}(\{x\}) = 0\).

But the space is discrete, so all of its mass sits on its atoms:

\[\sum _{x \in \mathcal {X}} \mathbb {P}(\{x\}) = 1\]

A countable sum of zeros cannot be one, and we have our contradiction.
